Most of the choice comes down to two questions: where is it going, and how much traffic will it take. Everything else is appearance.

Porcelain or ceramic

Porcelain is fired harder and denser than ordinary ceramic. It takes up less water, which is why it copes with a wet room and an outdoor step, and it chips less under a dropped pot. Ceramic is lighter, easier to cut, and perfectly good on a wall that nobody walks on.

Nearly everything we stock for floors is porcelain. Our wall ranges are the lighter body, which is what you want overhead and at eye level.

The finish is the decision you live with

Glazed
A coat of glass over the body. Colour and pattern are printed under it, so the range of looks is widest here. Easy to wipe, which is why it ends up in kitchens and sitting rooms. Our largest ranges are glazed porcelain in 600x600 and 600x1200.
Matt
No shine. It hides dust, water marks and footprints better than anything else, and it grips better underfoot when wet. If you have children, an outdoor edge, or a bathroom floor, this is the safe answer.
Polished
Ground to a mirror. It throws light around a room and makes a small space look bigger, and it is the most slippery thing in the catalogue once it is wet. Beautiful in a sitting room or a shop floor. Think twice for a bathroom, a kitchen or a veranda.
Matt textured
Matt with a surface you can feel. The most grip of the four. Ramps, entrances, anywhere that gets rained on.

A and B grade

Many of our ranges come in two grades. The B grade is the same tile at a lower price. If you are tiling a back room, a store or a rental, it is worth asking for. For a room you will look at every day, buy A grade and stop thinking about it.

Every B grade page on this site links to its A grade twin, and the other way round, so you can see both prices before deciding.

Buy the whole job at once

Tiles are made in batches, and two batches of the same code can differ slightly in shade. It is not a fault, it is how firing works. Order everything for a room in one go, including the spare, and lay from more than one box at a time so any variation is spread out rather than sitting in one corner.
