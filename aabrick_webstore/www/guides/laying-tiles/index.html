A tiled floor fails at the surface underneath it far more often than at the tile. Most of the work happens before the first tile goes down.

The surface

It has to be sound, clean, dry and flat. Sound means nothing moves or sounds hollow. Clean means no dust, no oil, no old paint or adhesive. Dry means a new screed has been given time, not just a warm afternoon.

Flat matters more the bigger the tile. Lay a straight edge across the floor and look for daylight under it. Fill the dips and grind the humps before you start, because a tile will not bridge them and a large one will rock.

Set out before you stick anything

Find the middle of the room and lay a dry run out from it, so the cuts land evenly at both walls rather than full tiles at one and slivers at the other. Check it against the most visible wall, which is usually the one you see when you walk in, and shift the whole grid if the cuts would fall badly there.

Rooms are rarely square. Work to a line you have snapped, not to the wall.

Adhesive

We stock Ultra Tile Fix 2, a cement based adhesive suitable for porcelain, granite and natural stone, for interior and exterior work, in 20kg bags. It is shrinkage compensated, which is what stops a large tile pulling as the adhesive cures.

Mix it to the bag, not by eye, and let it stand for the few minutes the bag asks for before using it. Comb it out with a notched trowel and lay while it is still tacky. If it has skinned over, comb it again.

Back butter large tiles: a skim on the back of the tile as well as the bed on the floor. You are aiming for full contact underneath. Lift the first tile back up and look at the coverage before you commit to the room.

Spacers, and lay from several boxes

Use spacers, even where the tile has a lug. Consistent joints are most of what makes a floor look professionally laid.

Open three or four boxes and take from all of them as you go. Shade varies slightly within a batch, and spreading it out is invisible while working from one box at a time leaves a patch.

Grout

Wait until the adhesive has properly set before grouting, which is longer than it looks. Grouting too early lifts tiles and traps moisture underneath.

Work the grout in diagonally across the joints so it packs rather than skating over, then clean off with a damp sponge, rinsing often. The haze that appears afterwards comes off with a dry cloth once it has dried, and it is much harder to remove a week later.

Leave movement joints where the floor meets a wall and at doorways. Silicone, not grout, in internal corners and where a floor meets a bath or a basin. Grout there will crack.

The mistakes that cost the most

  • Tiling over a moving floorEvery tile will crack along the same line
  • Too little adhesiveHollow spots break under a dropped pan or a chair leg
  • Grouting too earlyLifts tiles and traps water
  • Polished tile in a wet roomIt is genuinely dangerous underfoot
  • Running outThe next batch will not match. Order the spare up front
