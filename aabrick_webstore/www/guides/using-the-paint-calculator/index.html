Paint is bought in litres and sold in tins, and the gap between the two is where money gets wasted. This works out both, and takes the doors and windows off for you.

1. Give it the room, not the floor

This is the one that catches people. You are painting the walls, and the walls are the perimeter times the height, which is nothing like the floor area.

A room 5m by 4m has 20 square metres of floor. Its walls, at 3m high, come to 54. Someone who buys paint for 20 goes back to the shop twice.

So give it length, width and height and it does that sum. If you already know the wall area, the other button lets you type it in directly.

2. Say whether the ceiling is included

Walls only or walls and ceiling. The ceiling is the floor area, which is why it is only counted when you ask: most jobs do the ceiling in a different paint, often a different finish, and sometimes not at all.

3. Take off the doors and windows

Type how many there are. A standard door is about 1.8 square metres and a window about 1.44, and both figures can be changed if yours are bigger.

Painting a boundary wall or a ceiling with nothing in it? Set them to zero.

4. Say what you are painting onto

This is what decides how far a litre goes, and the difference is large.

  • 13 m²/LA repaint over sound, smooth emulsion
  • 11 m²/LNew plaster or a skimmed wall
  • 8.5 m²/LRough or textured plaster. Texture is surface area
  • 6.5 m²/LBare block or brick, which drinks it

If the tin is in front of you, use the number printed on it instead: choose I have the tin in front of me and type what it claims. These figures are the careful end of what manufacturers print, because running short mid wall is worse than having half a litre left.

5. Coats

Two is normal. Three when a pale colour is going over a dark one, or onto bare plaster. One is only ever a freshening up.

What it tells you

Litres, and then the tins that is, in the size you chose. The tins are rounded up once across the whole job rather than per coat, so you are not sold a tin nobody needs.

A worked example. That 5m by 4m room at 3m high: 54 square metres of wall, less a door and a window, leaves 50.76 to paint. Two coats at 13 square metres a litre is 7.8 litres, which is one 20 litre tin, or two 5 litre tins with a little spare.

Why there is no price

We do not sell paint, so there is nothing for us to quote and the cost line stays empty. Type in what a tin costs where you are buying it and it will work out the total for you.

Buy the whole job in one batch whatever you pay for it. Two tins of the same colour mixed months apart will not match on the wall.
