PVC ceiling boards are sold by the board, not by the square metre, so the only number that matters at the counter is how many boards. That is what this works out.

1. Measure the ceiling

A ceiling is the floor measured again, so length times width of the room. The buttons at the top let you type the metres or, if you already know the square metres, enter that straight in. Add another area handles a house a room at a time.

2. Choose the board

The list holds the sizes we stock, and picking one fills in its width, length and price.

  • 250mmIn 4m, 5m and 6m lengths
  • 300mmIn 4m, 5m and 6m lengths
  • Longer runsFewer joins across the ceiling, less waste

A board 250mm wide and 6m long covers 1.5 square metres. Pick the length that suits the room: a 6m board in a 5m room leaves a metre of offcut on every single board, so sometimes the shorter one is the cheaper job.

3. Set the wastage

10 per cent for a straight, simple ceiling. 15 per cent where there are bulkheads, a stepped ceiling, or an awkward shape, because every change of direction is another cut.

What it tells you

The boards to buy, and the working: the ceiling area, the area with wastage, what one board covers, and what the boards you are buying will cover.

A worked example. A room 5m by 4m is 20 square metres. Plus 10 per cent is 22. A 250mm by 6m board covers 1.5, and 22 divided by 1.5 is 14.7, so 15 boards.

The last line is the cost, and it is the boards multiplied by the price of a board. That matters: pricing the raw square metres instead would quote you less than the boards you have just been told to buy.

Before you order

The boards are not on the website yet, so the price here follows our shelf rate and is a guide. Ring your branch or send us the dimensions to have it confirmed, and ask at the same time about the strips and trim, which are not in this calculation.
